Questions we often get

How can I tell whether our delays come from the code?
These are the situations managers describe to us most often. If two or more sound familiar, the code is likely part of the problem.
  • Bugs your team fixed last month are back, and fixing them again eats the sprint
  • A small feature takes weeks, and nobody can explain why
  • Each release is followed by a few days of urgent fixes
  • Your team keeps asking for time to clean up the code
How does this help our dev team?
It takes work off their plate. We do the testing and the auditing, and with Rescue we also write the fixes, so your developers can stay on the roadmap. They get a clear list of what to fix, where it is and how long each fix takes, instead of discovering it one sprint at a time.
Our team is strong. Why bring in someone from outside?
Any team that works on the same code every day gets used to it and stops noticing the parts that slow it down. An outside review brings a fresh pair of eyes, for the same reason developers review each other's code. Your team keeps every decision and can question any finding with the engineer who wrote it.
Will it make our team faster?
Time in a slow codebase tends to go to the same places every sprint: bugs that come back, parts of the code everyone avoids, and releases checked by hand. Once those are fixed and covered by tests, each change takes less time to make and less time to check.
Do we have to stop building features during the Audit?
No. Keep in mind that a feature built on top of an unfixed problem makes that problem more expensive to fix later, so we suggest holding new features until the Critical items are done.
Will the app be bug-free afterwards?
No, and we say so up front. We fix every item in the report and check it against its condition. Code written after the rescue can bring new bugs, which is why every fix comes with a test.
What if fixing costs more than rewriting?
Then the report says so and puts both estimates side by side. Rewriting one part of the system is sometimes cheaper than patching it, and the decision stays with you.
Will I own the code?
Yes. Every fix goes into your own repository, and the report and documentation belong to you.
What happens after the rescue?
You can stop there, or move to Tech Labs and keep building with the developers who already know your codebase.
