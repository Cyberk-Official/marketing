"Problem & Solution
"	1. What specific problem are you trying to solve? Who is struggling with this problem?	When a lead comes in, whoever calls back first usually wins it. Real estate agents who respond within 5 minutes are 21 times more likely to qualify that lead than one who waits 30, and insurance agents see roughly a 9x lift doing the same. Individual agents and small agencies can't hit that window on every lead themselves, that gap is the problem.
	2. How are people currently solving this problem?	Right now it's an agent trying to call back fast themselves, or a brokerage hiring an inside sales rep to triage leads, and neither covers nights, weekends, or the exact minute a lead lands. By one estimate, 71% of internet leads are wasted from poor follow-up alone
	3. What makes your solution different or better?	Honest update after checking: real estate already has dedicated tools doing qualification, follow-up, and booking together, so "does both directions" isn't as unique here as it looked before. The sharper angle worth testing is insurance and property agents served together in one product, since nothing found so far does that, plus pricing built for a single agent rather than a brokerage-wide sale.
	4. Can you describe your product/service in 2-3 sentences?	Early-stage concept: an AI phone agent built for insurance agents, property agents, and small sales teams, calling or answering within moments of a lead coming in instead of hours later. It qualifies the lead, books the appointment straight into the calendar, and follows up automatically if nobody picks up, all logged to one record, though exact scope is still being shaped.
"Target Customers
"	5. Who is your ideal customer (age, occupation, habits, location)?	Insurance agents and agencies, property agents and agencies, and small B2B sales teams, exactly the buyer profile where speed to lead is a proven, measured lever rather than a nice-to-have. Singapore is the confirmed first market, so licensing rules for both insurance intermediaries and CEA-registered property agents need to shape what the AI is actually allowed to say on a call.
	6. Have you talked to any potential customers yet? What was their feedback?	Conversations have already happened with potential clients in this space. The response is good so far
	7. How big do you estimate this market to be?	The AI voice agent market is around $2.5 billion now, heading toward $35 billion by 2033, growing roughly 39% a year. Real estate and insurance specifically already have several dedicated players building exactly this, which confirms the demand but also means it's not an empty field to walk into.
Business Model	8. How do you plan to make money (direct sales, subscription, commission, advertising...)?	Monthly subscription, likely per agent or per seat given individual insurance and property agents are the buyer, Subscription per office worth exploring. Usage-based overage above a call volume threshold is worth adding once real data exists.
	9. What price point do you have in mind? What is it based on?	First pass: $79 to $300 a month per agent, which lines up with what similar SMB voice AI tools charge, roughly $49 to $499 a month. That range skews US-centric though, worth sanity-checking against what a Singapore insurance or property agent would actually pay before locking it in.
	10. Who are your main competitors?	For real estate specifically: NLPearl and ContactSwing already do qualification, follow-up, and booking together. For general SMB reception or outbound, Goodcall and Regal.ai are the closest comps, though nothing found so far specifically targets insurance and property agents together.
Product & Project Scope	11. What features are must-haves for the first version (MVP)?	Call back a new lead within the first minute, qualify it on that call, and book straight into the calendar, that's the core loop. Add a no-code setup, one shared record per lead, human handoff when it gets complex, low latency response from the AI and adaptable language change.
	12. Which features can be saved for a later phase?	Text fallback when a call doesn't connect, deeper CRM integrations (Salesforce, HubSpot), call quality scoring, and multi-agent routing can wait for later. Cold outbound to people with zero prior contact stays off the table for now, given how much stricter consent rules get there.

Expectations & Success	18. What does success look like to you after 6 months / 1 year?	Nearly every lead called back inside the target window, a real lift in qualified-lead rate for the agents using it, and retention month over month, that last one still matters most. Given the speed-to-lead numbers above, a good six-month story is agents converting meaningfully more of the same leads they were already paying for.
	19. What concerns you most about this project?	Leading platforms are hitting around 280 milliseconds end to end in 2026, with anything under 300ms feeling conversational rather than robotic. The fix is the same one budget and timeline already point to, build on proven voice infrastructure instead of a custom pipeline.
